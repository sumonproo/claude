Real estate guide: industrial land and buildings

Most manufacturers lease space in an industrial park, and some buy land. Here is how foreign ownership works and what to check before signing.

Last reviewed November 2026. Written with contributors from our experts directory.

  2. Leasing in an industrial park

Foreign ownership rules

Foreigners cannot directly own land in the restricted zone: within 100 km of the borders and 50 km of the coast. Within that zone, residential property can be held through a bank trust (fideicomiso). Mexican companies with foreign shareholders can own land there for non-residential use, such as a factory, after notifying the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Outside the restricted zone, Mexican companies with foreign investment can own land directly.

Leasing in an industrial park

  • Leases are often priced in US dollars, with an annual rent increase.
  • Terms usually run from 5 to 10 years for standard buildings, longer for build-to-suit projects.
  • Many leases are net leases, where the tenant also pays property tax, insurance and maintenance.
  • Expect a security deposit, and sometimes a parent company guarantee.

Checks before you buy or lease

  • A title search and certificate of no liens from the Public Registry of Property.
  • Land use and zoning permits that allow your activity.
  • Whether the land was ever communal (ejido) land, which needs proper regularization.
  • Electricity capacity from the utility (CFE), water supply and wastewater permits.
  • Environmental condition of the site.

Purchase costs and taxes

Buyers pay a real estate acquisition tax set by each state or municipality, plus notary fees and registration costs. Owners pay an annual property tax (predial).

Common questions

Can my foreign company buy land in Mexico?

Not directly in the restricted zone. A Mexican subsidiary can own land for non-residential use, including within the restricted zone after notifying the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Are industrial leases in pesos or dollars?

Industrial park leases are commonly priced in US dollars. Peso leases are possible, and the currency is worth negotiating.

What is a build-to-suit lease?

The developer builds a facility to your specifications and you lease it, usually on a longer term than a standard building.
