International trade guide: importing and exporting

What companies need to import into and export from Mexico: registrations, customs clearance, taxes on imports, and how trade agreements lower tariffs.

Registrations you need

  • An active tax ID (RFC) and electronic signature (e.firma) with the tax authority (SAT).
  • Registration in the importers' registry (Padrón de Importadores), required for most commercial imports.
  • A sector registry (padrón sectorial) for certain goods, such as steel, textiles, footwear and some chemicals.
  • For a small number of goods, a sector registry for exporters as well.

Customs clearance and brokers

Commercial imports and exports are cleared through a customs declaration (pedimento), filed in practice by a licensed customs broker (agente aduanal). The importer authorizes the broker electronically for each shipment or for a period.

The importer stays responsible for what is declared: the tariff classification, the customs value and the country of origin. Errors can lead to fines and back duties, so review your broker's classifications before your first shipments.

Taxes and fees on imports

  • General import duty (IGI): set by the product's tariff code and its country of origin.
  • VAT: 16%, calculated on the customs value plus duty.
  • Customs processing fee (DTA) on each declaration.
  • Excise tax (IEPS) on some goods, such as fuels, alcohol and tobacco.

In recent years Mexico has raised import duties on many products from countries without a trade agreement with Mexico, including China. Check the current rate for your tariff code before you set prices.

If you import inputs to manufacture goods for export, the IMMEX program lets you defer duty and VAT on them.

Trade agreements

Mexico has free trade agreements covering about 50 countries, including the USMCA (known in Mexico as T-MEC) with the United States and Canada, the agreement with the European Union, and the CPTPP with Pacific countries.

To pay a lower preferential duty, goods must meet the agreement's rules of origin, and the importer must hold valid proof of origin. Plan your supply chain around these rules if you sell into the US or Canada.

Standards and permits

Many products must meet Mexican official standards (NOMs), especially for labeling, safety and energy use, before they can be sold. Some goods also need an import permit or approval from a specific authority, such as the Ministry of Economy, the health regulator (COFEPRIS) or the agriculture authority (SENASICA).

Exporting from Mexico

Exports are generally not subject to export duties and are zero-rated for VAT. For definitive exports, the exporter issues an electronic invoice (CFDI) with the foreign trade complement, which customs matches against the export declaration.

Common questions

Do I need to register to import into Mexico?

Yes. Most commercial imports require registration in the importers' registry with SAT, and some goods also need a sector registry.

Can I import without a customs broker?

In practice, almost all commercial shipments are cleared through a licensed customs broker, who files the customs declaration for you.

Does Mexico have a trade agreement with China?

No. Goods from China pay Mexico's general import duties, which have increased for many products in recent years.
