Corporate guide: setting up a company in Mexico

Most foreign investors operate through a Mexican company. This guide covers the common entity types, the incorporation steps and what to keep up with afterwards.

Last reviewed November 2026. Written with contributors from our experts directory.

On this page

  1. Common entity types
  2. Incorporation steps
  3. Documents from foreign shareholders

  5. Common questions

Common entity types

  • S.A. de C.V. (sociedad anónima de capital variable): a corporation with at least two shareholders. The most widely used form.
  • S. de R.L. de C.V. (sociedad de responsabilidad limitada): a limited liability company with two to 50 partners. Often chosen by US parents for tax reasons.
  • Branch: a registered branch of the foreign company. Less common, because the parent stays directly liable.

There is no legal minimum capital for these companies. The amount is set in the bylaws.

Incorporation steps

  1. Get authorization for the company name from the Ministry of Economy.
  2. Sign the bylaws before a notary public or commercial broker.
  3. Register the company in the Public Registry of Commerce.
  4. Register with SAT to get a tax ID (RFC) and electronic signature (e.firma).
  5. Register foreign shareholders in the National Registry of Foreign Investment (RNIE).
  6. Open a bank account.
  7. Register as an employer with social security (IMSS) and for state payroll tax before hiring.

Documents from foreign shareholders

Foreign shareholders usually need apostilled and translated powers of attorney, proof of their own incorporation, and a foreign tax ID. The company also needs a legal representative in Mexico who has their own RFC.

Ongoing compliance

  • Monthly tax filings and electronic invoices (CFDI) for every sale.
  • An annual tax return and an annual shareholders' meeting.
  • Updated corporate books and reports to the foreign investment registry.
  • Records identifying the company's beneficial owners.
  • Transfer pricing documentation for transactions with related companies.

Common questions

How long does incorporation take?

Usually a few weeks. Opening the bank account and completing tax registration are often the slowest steps.

Can a company have only foreign shareholders?

In most sectors, yes. A few activities are reserved for Mexican nationals or have foreign ownership limits.

Do I need a local director?

You need a legal representative in Mexico with a tax ID to sign filings and contracts. Directors themselves do not have to be Mexican.
